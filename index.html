Academic Foundation and Early Engineering Journey

My path in computer science began with my undergraduate studies at Biju Patnaik University of Technology (BPUT) in Rourkela, Odisha, where I earned my Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) in Computer Science and Engineering (2011 to 2017) with a 6.13 CGPA. During these formative years, I developed a strong interest in how data moves across distributed networks and how software interfaces with underlying hardware.

To see how large-scale communications operated outside the textbook, I completed specialized vocational training early in my academic career. From May 12, 2014, to June 8, 2014, I trained at the BSNL Old Telephone Exchange in Hazaribagh, Jharkhand, focusing on Telecommunication Networking. There, I gained direct exposure to Packet Switching, Telephone Switching, and Printed Circuit Board (PCB) Control. This experience demystified physical network infrastructure for me and inspired my undergraduate capstone project.

For my B.Tech project at BPUT, I developed "A Novel Framework on Showing The Flow of Packet Blocks Using Packet Switching." The goal of this web-application-based project was to make an abstract networking concept visible and intuitive. In standard networking courses, students often struggle to visualize how data is segmented, routed across varying paths, and reassembled. I built a system that demonstrated the transmission of data across the internet and local PCs by breaking information down into small packet blocks. The application tracked and displayed the live flow of these packet blocks on a PC screen as they took different network paths to reach their final destination.

Before completing my undergraduate degree, I also expanded my software development skills through vocational training at the BSNL Advanced Regional Telecom Training Centre (ARTTC) in Ranchi, Jharkhand, from January 23, 2017, to February 18, 2017. Concentrating on ASP.NET Vocational Training, I learned full-stack website development using CSS3, HTML5, and the .NET framework, giving me a solid grasp of web architecture that would later inform my work in web and server security.

Advanced Postgraduate Studies and Machine Learning Research

After building a foundation in networking, web technologies, and cryptography (including completing Cryptography Fundamentals through Cybrary on December 20, 2019), I decided to deepen my research capabilities by pursuing a postgraduate degree. From 2020 to 2022, I attended Mahatma Gandhi Central University in East Champaran, Motihari, Bihar, where I earned my Master of Technology (M.Tech) in Computer Science and Engineering with a 6.95 CGPA.

My M.Tech studies marked a major turning point in my career. It was here that I brought together my two primary technical interests: cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. Traditional signature-based antivirus tools often fall behind when confronted with obfuscated code or new variants of malicious scripts. I wanted to explore how data science and natural language processing techniques could be adapted to spot threats hidden inside text and code files.

My M.Tech thesis project, titled "Virus Detection on Text Files Using Text Mining: Cybersecurity Using Machine Learning," tackled this exact problem. In this project, I designed an analytical pipeline to inspect text files and determine whether they contained malicious virus payloads. The workflow began by importing raw text files and transforming the unstructured text data into a structured mathematical representation using Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) in matrix format. Using Python feature extraction techniques, I isolated the relevant patterns and fed the resulting matrices into five distinct machine learning classifiers to evaluate and compare their detection accuracy.

This postgraduate research produced strong empirical results and led directly to my peer-reviewed publication. On March 29, 2023, my paper, titled "Code Text-Based Virus and Non-virus Classification and Comparative Analysis of Machine Learning Algorithms" (Paper ID 9530), was published in the proceedings of the 1st International Conference on Recent Trends and Future Trends in Smart Electronics System and Manufacturing (SESMIC 2022). Published by River Publishers (Book ID 1044, Article No. 13, DOI: 10.13052/rp-9788770229852), this study provided a comparative benchmark of how different machine learning algorithms perform when classifying viral versus benign code text. Maintaining active scholarly profiles on Google Scholar and ORCID (0009-0001-0532-6750), I continue to build upon this research as I advance through my doctoral program at NIAMT Ranchi.
